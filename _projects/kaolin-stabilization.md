## Research context and objectives

Low strength and moisture-sensitive behavior can limit the suitability of fine-grained soils for subgrades, embankments, and other earthworks. Chemical stabilization can improve these properties, but its effectiveness depends on the interaction between soil mineralogy, binder composition, activation conditions, and curing time.

This study investigated **ladle refining furnace (LRF) slag**, a by-product of secondary steel refining, as a stabilizing material for kaolin clay. Although rich in calcium and containing silica and alumina, LRF slag has relatively limited intrinsic reactivity. The central question was whether sodium hydroxide activation, combined with lime, could improve its binding performance and produce a more stable soil matrix.

The investigation compared individual and combined treatments to examine three related questions:

- How do lime, untreated slag, and activated slag affect compaction and strength development?
- Does the treatment that maximizes strength also provide the greatest reduction in swelling?
- What microstructural and compositional changes help explain the measured engineering response?

## My contribution

I contributed to **data analysis and interpretation, manuscript writing, and review**. The experimental design and laboratory investigation formed part of a collaborative study with Arnob Sutradhar as part of the Geotechnical Engineering Research Group at the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering, SUST.

## Experimental and analytical approach

The experimental program compared six treatment groups: untreated kaolin, lime-treated kaolin, untreated-slag mixtures, activated-slag mixtures, lime–slag mixtures, and lime–activated-slag mixtures. The investigated binder contents included 3%, 5%, and 8% lime and 15%, 20%, and 25% slag, with NaOH used to prepare the activated slag.

Compaction testing established the maximum dry density and optimum moisture content of the mixtures. Unconfined compressive strength (UCS) was evaluated after 7, 14, and 28 days of curing, using three specimens per mixture at each curing age. Free-swell testing assessed changes in swelling behavior.

Scanning electron microscopy coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM–EDS) was used to examine selected mixtures after 28 days. These observations supported interpretation of particle arrangement, matrix densification, and changes in elemental composition.

The study also used multivariate regression to examine associations between mixture composition, curing duration, and UCS.

## Principal findings

### Activation substantially improved strength development

The highest reported 28-day UCS was **4,619.8 kPa**, achieved by the mixture containing **8% lime and 20% activated slag**, compared with **63.44 kPa** for untreated kaolin.

The comparison between treatment groups clarifies the importance of activation and binder combination:

| Treatment | Representative mixture | Reported 28-day UCS |
|---|---|---:|
| Untreated kaolin | No stabilizer | 63.44 kPa |
| Lime alone | 8% lime | 658.2 kPa |
| Untreated LRF slag | 20% slag | 106.1 kPa |
| Activated LRF slag | 20% activated slag | 2,946.5 kPa |
| Lime and untreated slag | 8% lime + 20% slag | 1,600.9 kPa |
| Lime and activated slag | 8% lime + 20% activated slag | 4,619.8 kPa |

These results indicate that slag content alone does not explain the strength improvement. Chemical activation and the combined lime–activated-slag treatment were central to the observed response.

### Strength and swelling required separate evaluation

Treatment reduced the reported free-swell index from 2.7 cm³/g for untreated kaolin to minimum values of 1.6 cm³/g for activated-slag mixtures and 1.8 cm³/g for lime–activated-slag mixtures.

The combined treatment therefore produced the highest strength but did not achieve the lowest swelling. This distinction highlights the need to evaluate stabilization against multiple performance criteria rather than selecting a mixture solely from its UCS.

### Microstructural observations supported the engineering trends

SEM observations showed a transition from loosely arranged particles toward a denser, more strongly bonded matrix. Changes in elemental ratios were consistent with the formation of calcium- and sodium-bearing aluminosilicate reaction products.

The paper interprets these observations as indirect evidence of cementitious gel formation. SEM–EDS alone does not definitively identify individual mineral phases; complementary XRD and FTIR analyses are needed to confirm the proposed reaction products and possible expansive phases.

### Statistical relationships were informative but conditional

The regression model explained approximately 73% of the variation in UCS within the investigated dataset, with lime and activated-slag contents showing strong associations with strength development.

Because mixture proportions are interdependent, the predictors exhibited multicollinearity. The coefficients should consequently be interpreted as conditional associations within the fitted model, rather than independent causal effects or a universally applicable design equation.

## Significance and limitations

The study demonstrates the laboratory potential of a lime–activated-LRF-slag binder for improving kaolin clay while identifying a useful tradeoff between strength gain and swelling reduction. It also illustrates the value of connecting mechanical testing with microstructural observations and statistical interpretation.

The strongest mixture is an optimum within the investigated materials, proportions, and curing conditions, rather than a general field recommendation. Transfer to natural soils requires evaluation of mineralogical variability, slag consistency, construction conditions, and longer-term behavior.

Further work should examine wetting–drying durability, sulfate exposure, leaching, activator optimization, and field performance. The reuse of an industrial by-product motivates the research, but the study does not establish a quantified life-cycle carbon benefit or comprehensive environmental safety.
